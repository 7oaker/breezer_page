**Short version: the difference is not quality, it is the product.** Smoke Free is an established stop-smoking app with a long development history and a foundation in behaviour change research. It is built for cigarettes. Breezer is built for nicotine pouches and starts at tracking rather than at a quit date.

In fairness: this page is published by Breezer, so we are not neutral. What it says about Smoke Free is therefore deliberately limited to what follows from the app's public positioning, as of **18 August 2026**. Features and prices change; check the current listing if it matters.

## The unit is the problem

For pouch users this is the practical core, and it holds regardless of how good a stop-smoking app is.

A cigarette app counts in cigarettes not smoked, in pack prices and in lung recovery. Your unit is a pouch. Your strength is printed in milligrams per pouch, not in tar. And the recovery that concerns you happens in your mouth, not your lungs.

That is not a detail in daily use. You end up converting constantly, inside an app whose numbers never described your habit, and the milestones meant to motivate you describe something that is not happening to you.

Worth noting in the same breath: the usual health milestones for pouches are themselves adapted from tobacco cessation, and recovery in the mouth is barely researched for pouches specifically. We say so on our own [quit snus](/quit-snus) page rather than glossing over it.

## Where Smoke Free is strong

Smoke Free has earned its reputation. It has been developed for over a decade, is grounded in behaviour change research, and its paid tiers go further than is usual in this category, up to access to counselling.

That last point matters more than it sounds, and it counts in Smoke Free's favour. The Cochrane review on smokeless tobacco finds a risk ratio of **1.76** for **behavioural counselling** against minimal support, while for smartphone apps as such the evidence stays open (RR 1.00 at very low certainty, meaning undecided). An app that builds in real counsellor contact is reaching for the thing that actually works. The numbers and sources are on [quit snus](/quit-snus).

If you smoke cigarettes and want a serious programme, we would recommend Smoke Free over Breezer. That is not politeness, it is the better fit.

## Where Breezer fits

**Pouches, not cigarettes.** One tap logs one pouch, brand-agnostic across [Zyn](/zyn-tracker), Velo, LOOP and the rest. The breakdowns are built for pouch counts rather than converted from cigarettes.

**Tracking without having decided yet.** Breezer does not require a quit date. You can count for weeks, look at your own numbers, and switch on [Quit Mode](/quit-snus) later. Very few people go from full use to zero in one step, and an app that assumes it loses exactly the people who are still at the start.

**Friends, not a chart.** In Breezer, friends, rankings and the leaderboard are part of the core features rather than sitting behind a paywall. When a friend takes a pouch, you see it, which turns a private habit into a visible one.

That this does something is not just our opinion. The Cochrane review [Group behaviour therapy programmes for smoking cessation](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:db763ebe-3234-4741-8e7f-de4867e3d0f7) by Stead, Carroll and Lancaster (2017) pooled 66 trials and found group programmes clearly ahead of self-help material, at a risk ratio of **1.88** (95% CI 1.52–2.33). The honest caveat belongs with it: against one-to-one counselling of the same intensity, the group does **not** win (RR 0.99). So it is not the group as such, it is committed support. A friends list is the low-threshold version of that, not a replacement for counselling.

## Which should you choose?

**Smoke Free**, if you smoke cigarettes, have committed to quitting, and want a structured programme with optional professional coaching.

**Breezer**, if your habit is nicotine pouches, you want to see your real use before deciding, or you want people alongside rather than only a chart.

Strictly speaking the two are not competing for the same person. And for both: an app is a measuring instrument and a prompt, not a treatment. Anyone serious about quitting should add real support; most countries run a free quitline.
